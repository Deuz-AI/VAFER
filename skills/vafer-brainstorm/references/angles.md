# Angles

Choose the four details (the person, far field, horizon and what a route means) together, so no two angles return the same points.

## The brief

`{angle, filled}` is the angle's name, job and detail below; Blind spots also gets the checklist. An angle's own count replaces "4-5". Already known opens with the step 1 goal, marked (guess). Keep the word brainstorm out of every filled brief, summary included; call this skill the idea module.

```text
Today is {date}. A person asked an assistant:
"""{request, minus any call to brainstorm}"""
Already known (their framing, not settled truth): {at most 120 words, or "only the request"}
Your angle: {angle, filled}. Others cover: {four names}; stay on yours.
First line "OBVIOUS:" and five bare titles any assistant would give here. Then 4-5 points that are neither obvious nor already known, one line each:
point | why here | changes: which choice, from what to what | first step, under an hour | p: chance a typical assistant raises it
At least half need p below 0.10. Drop a point that would fit another request with the nouns swapped, or whose "changes" you cannot fill. Tag any number, law, price, product or date you state [fact]. Last line "PREMISE:" and why the request aims at the wrong thing, or "none". No praise, no summary.
Reply in {language}, at most 180 words. Use only what you know: no web, no files. Do not invoke skills or start other agents.
```

## The five angles

- **Routes**: 3–5 routes to the goal by different means: much smaller, much bigger, buy it, or do nothing. For a request for ideas, 4–6 ideas; for a choice, only routes that are neither option. Detail: what a route is (research: another framing; page: a page concept; figure: an object whose motion fits the idea).
- **Blind spots**: checklist items the request is silent on that matter here, other than alternatives, failure modes and the first encounter; last, the plan if the weakest assumption is wrong.
- **Failure**: it failed, told as certain: first warning signs and one failure born of success; for a choice, each option in turn. Detail: the horizon.
- **One person**: first encounter with the result: what they want, what stops them, what they do instead. Detail: an ordinary person the request says least about.
- **Far field**: 2–3 mechanisms from a distant field that solved this structural problem; no named cases or years. Detail: the far field; the structural problem, briefly.

Checklist (what requests often leave out): the goal and its why; who it is for, decides and pays; how and when success is checked; constraints (budget, time, skills, platform, language); current practice and prior art; alternatives (doing nothing, buying, a smaller version); risks and failure modes; assumptions that must hold; dependencies (people, data, systems, approvals); legal, safety and privacy; second-order effects (downstream, at scale, over time).

## Deepen

A deepen brief is the angle brief with lines 4–7 replaced by these, Decided lines added to what is known, and its own word limit.

Developer, one per pick, at most 100 words:

```text
They picked: {the pick}.
Make it concrete: the steps, a one-week test and the result that would refute it, and tomorrow's first task. Tag any number, law, price, product or date you state [fact]. No praise, no summary.
```

Counter agent, one for all picks, at most 60 words per pick:

```text
They lean toward: {the picks}.
Make the strongest case that it is wrong, does not matter or aims at the wrong goal: the best counter-example you know, what it costs that they have not counted, and the one condition under which it still holds. Do not balance or concede.
```
